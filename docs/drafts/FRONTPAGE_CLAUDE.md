# boat-agent

**A deckhand with perfect memory. Talks back. Never sleeps. Works on your boat today.**

---

You're on the back deck, running gear, gloves on, fish slime to your elbows. Mid-day, mid-set, you don't stop. You just say it:

**"Twelve chum over the rail. Two of them bruisers."**

And you go back to work.

A week later you're idling in the same water, coffee in hand, and you ask:
**"How'd we do here last Tuesday?"**

It tells you. What you caught, when the bite turned, what the tide was doing, what the sounder looked like — because it wrote it all down, when it happened, where it happened. Not in some cloud account. On your boat, in files named with the time and the place, which is why "last Tuesday off the point" is a question it can actually answer.

That's level one: **a voice chatbot with perfect memory, built for commercial fishing.**

You never touch a keyboard. You never name a file. You never save a log. It just remembers.

The AI model doesn't matter. The memory is the product, and the memory is yours. Even if you never turn on anything else on this page, that alone earns its keep.

---

## Level 2 — It watches the sounder

You know how you stare at the sounder all day, keeping the picture in your head?

It does that now. Every minute it notes what changed. Every ten minutes it writes a full record. Every hour, a summary. Every evening, a daily debrief — what the grounds looked like, where the marks were, what shifted, when the bait came through.

The debrief is nice. But here's the part that matters:

**All of it goes into the same memory as your conversations.**

So next season you can ask "when did we see bait stacked at thirty fathoms?" and get an answer with the day, the time, and the spot — because the sounder watch and your voice notes are one searchable pile, grouped by place. You're not keeping a logbook anymore. You're just fishing, and the logbook writes itself.

---

## Level 3 — More eyes

Bolt on a cheap camera and it watches what you can't:

- **Forward camera** — logs, buoys, boats coming at you in the dark. A second set of eyes on the bow when you're on the gear, not the water ahead.
- **Engine-room camera (infrared)** — hot spots before they're failures, a leak before it's a story, bilge level creeping up at 3 AM at the dock. It texts you.

Same memory. Same conversation. "Any alarms last night?" is a question you can ask over coffee.

---

## Level 4 — Say the word

**"Take the helm."** It steers.

**"I've got her."** You're driving again.

The wheel and the throttle are always yours first — the system asks, it never insists, and your hand on the lever outranks everything it thinks. Every time. That's not a setting. That's the design.

---

## Level 5 — It builds things with you

This is the part that sounds like next year but works today.

Say you want the throttle to handle itself while you troll — easing up when you turn into the wind, back down when you fall off.

You don't buy a system. You have a conversation:

*"What throttle are you running — cable, hydraulic, Micro Commander, chain and sprocket? How long's the run from the helm to the engine? What's the throw on the lever?"*

It interviews you like a sharp deckhand would. Then the two of you sketch the plan together. It tells you which servo and which parts to order and roughly what they'll cost. When the box arrives, it hands you a wiring diagram written for YOUR boat and the code to match — code you can read, in plain English, with the reasons written in.

You wrench it in. You're the hands; it's the head. The project is a collaboration between you and the computer, and the result is a boat that trolls the way YOU troll — because it learned from watching you do it.

And it remembers how it built it. Next season, when something needs adjusting, you ask.

---

## Install

1. **Install the app** on the boat's laptop (Windows, one installer).
2. **Plug in your GPS** (and the sounder screen, when you're ready for Level 2).
3. **Say something.** "Start the log." That's it — it's remembering.

Cameras, the autopilot, and the building-stuff-together part come later, when you want them. Everything works offline; the cloud is a backup, not a requirement.
